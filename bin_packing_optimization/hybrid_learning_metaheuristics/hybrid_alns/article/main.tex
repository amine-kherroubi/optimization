\begin{abstract}
	The one-dimensional bin packing problem (1D-BPP) is a strongly NP-hard
	combinatorial optimization problem requiring a set of $n$ weighted items to be
	assigned to the minimum number of capacity-$C$ bins.  Metaheuristics scale to
	large instances but rely on hand-crafted operators governed by policies that are
	fixed prior to execution and remain insensitive to the evolving state of the
	search.  We propose a dual-learning Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS)
	that embeds machine learning at two structurally distinct decision points within
	a single destroy--repair loop.  At the \emph{online} level, a warm-start
	LinUCB contextual bandit selects the destroy operator from a five-dimensional
	encoding of the current search state, replacing the static roulette-wheel
	mechanism of standard ALNS.  At the \emph{offline} level, a gradient-boosted
	trees (GBT) model---trained by behavioral cloning of Best-Fit Decreasing
	(BFD)---ranks feasible bins during repair, injecting instance-class knowledge
	without incurring online learning overhead.  An ablation study isolates each
	component's contribution across five standard benchmark families (Scholl,
	Falkenauer, W\"ascher, Hard28, $685$ instances total) under a budget of
	$5{,}000$ ALNS iterations. The ablation differentiates the four configurations
	in a dataset-dependent way: the dual-learning method delivers the lowest mean
	gap on Scholl-2 ($0.565$ bins versus $0.590$ for no-learning), the online
	bandit alone is strongest on Falkenauer-T triplets ($1.288$ vs $1.512$ bins,
	a $15\%$ reduction), and the offline repair model alone is strongest on
	Falkenauer-U ($0.738$ bins). In the comparative study the dual-learning ALNS
	achieves the lowest mean gap on the Scholl-2 slice ($0.05$ bins, versus
	$0.25$ for ant colony optimization at approximately $22\times$ the runtime),
	strictly dominating every classical baseline on solution quality. Across the
	five full benchmark families the combined method reaches the
	$\mathrm{LB}_1$-optimal solution on $80.4\%$ of Scholl-2 and $52.5\%$ of
	Falkenauer-U instances.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The one-dimensional bin packing problem (1D-BPP) is a classical combinatorial
optimization problem with broad industrial relevance, from logistics and
cutting-stock to scheduling and cloud resource allocation. Given $n$ items, each
with a positive integer size, and an unlimited supply of identical bins of fixed
capacity $C$, the task is to assign every item to a bin without exceeding capacity
while using as few bins as possible. Despite its simple statement, 1D-BPP is
strongly NP-hard~\cite{bGarey}, so exact solvers do not scale to large instances
and practitioners rely on heuristics and metaheuristics.

Two families dominate practical 1D-BPP solving. Constructive heuristics such as
First-Fit Decreasing (FFD) and Best-Fit Decreasing (BFD) run in $O(n\log n)$ and
offer bounded worst-case guarantees---FFD uses at most $\tfrac{11}{9}\,\mathrm{OPT}+\tfrac{6}{9}$
bins~\cite{bJohnson}---but they produce a single deterministic packing with no
further improvement. Metaheuristics---simulated annealing~\cite{bKirkpatrick},
tabu search~\cite{bGlover}, grouping genetic algorithms~\cite{bFalkenauer}, and
ant colony optimization~\cite{bDorigo}---explore the solution space iteratively
and reach higher-quality packings on hard instances. Their effectiveness,
however, hinges on manually designed neighborhood operators and on
selection and acceptance policies whose parameters are fixed in advance and
applied identically to every instance and at every stage of the search.
Crucially, classical metaheuristics do not exploit the information generated
during the search itself: neither the time-varying usefulness of an operator nor
the structural regularities of good placements are ever learned.

Machine learning (ML) offers a principled way to make these decisions
data-driven. Following the taxonomy of Bengio \textit{et al.}~\cite{bBengio}, ML
can be hybridized with metaheuristics in three broad modes: (i) predicting
algorithm configuration or parameters offline, (ii) guiding search decisions
online from the current state, and (iii) replacing a hand-crafted operator with a
learned model. The first mode adapts poorly within a single run, and the third
can be data- and compute-intensive in isolation. We therefore combine the second
and third modes at the two decisions that most influence ALNS quality: which part
of the incumbent solution to destroy, and how to rebuild it.

Learning-augmented metaheuristics have shown strong results on routing and
packing problems. Reinforcement learning has been used to select low-level
heuristics in hyper-heuristic frameworks, and neural models have been embedded in
large neighborhood search---for example Neural LNS for vehicle
routing~\cite{bHottung}---to learn destruction or repair policies. Contextual
bandits, in particular LinUCB~\cite{bLi,bChu}, provide a lightweight online
mechanism for adaptive operator selection with theoretical regret guarantees,
while gradient-boosted trees~\cite{bFriedman} are strong tabular rankers
well suited to scoring candidate placements. These ingredients have rarely been
combined within a single bin-packing solver.

This paper presents a dual-learning ALNS for 1D-BPP that injects learning at both
decision points of the destroy--repair loop. First, an online warm-start LinUCB
contextual bandit selects, at every iteration, one of three destroy operators
from a five-dimensional description of the current search state. Second, an
offline gradient-boosted-trees model, trained by behavioral cloning of BFD, ranks
the feasible bins for each displaced item during repair. The two components are
independent and can be toggled separately, which lets us isolate their individual
and joint contributions through an ablation study. To our knowledge, this
two-point integration---online operator selection together with offline learned
repair---has not been reported for bin packing.

Section~\ref{sec:formulation} formalizes the problem,

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

Let $\mathcal{I}=\{1,\dots,n\}$ be a set of items with strictly positive
integer sizes $s_i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, and let each bin have integer capacity
$C\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ with $s_i\le C$ for all $i\in\mathcal{I}$.  Introduce
binary decision variables $x_{ij}=1$ if item $i$ is placed in bin $j$, and
$y_j=1$ if bin $j$ is used, where $j\in\{1,\dots,n\}$.  The 1D-BPP is then:
%
\begin{align}
	\min \quad       & \sum_{j=1}^{n} y_j                                                      \label{eq:obj}                                                   \\
	\text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_{ij}=1,                                                               &  & \forall\,i\in\mathcal{I}   \label{eq:assign} \\
	                 & \sum_{i=1}^{n} s_i\,x_{ij}\le C\,y_j,                                                  &  & \forall\,j                 \label{eq:cap}    \\
	                 & x_{ij}\le y_j,\; x_{ij},y_j\in\{0,1\}.                                                 &  & \label{eq:link}
\end{align}
%
Constraint~\eqref{eq:assign} enforces that each item is assigned to exactly one
bin.  Constraint~\eqref{eq:cap} simultaneously bounds bin load by $C$ and
couples assignment variables to bin-usage indicators.  Constraint~\eqref{eq:link}
prevents an item from being placed in a bin that is not open.
Objective~\eqref{eq:obj} minimizes the number of bins used.

A standard continuous lower bound is obtained by noting that the total item
volume must be distributed over bins of capacity $C$, requiring at least
$\sum_i s_i / C$ bins; integrality gives
%
\begin{equation}
	\mathrm{LB}_1=\left\lceil \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} s_i}{C} \right\rceil.
	\label{eq:lb}
\end{equation}
%
Sharper lower bounds exist---notably the $L_2$ bound of Martello and
Toth~\cite{bMartello}, which exploits item-size interactions---but
$\mathrm{LB}_1$ suffices as the progress indicator used throughout this work.
Strong NP-hardness follows from a polynomial reduction from
3-Partition~\cite{bGarey}, establishing that no pseudo-polynomial exact
algorithm exists unless P$=$NP and justifying a metaheuristic treatment for
large instances.

\subsection{Solution Encoding and Warm Start}
A solution is represented as the bijection from items to bins, maintained as
two parallel arrays: for each item, the index of its assigned bin, and for each
bin, its current aggregate load.  Any removal or reinsertion updates both arrays
in $O(1)$, and the objective value---the count of non-empty bins---is read
directly from the bin-load array without additional traversal.

The search is initialized with Best-Fit Decreasing (BFD).  Items are processed
in non-increasing size order; each item is placed in the open bin whose
residual capacity after placement is minimized, and a new bin is opened only
when no feasible bin exists.  BFD's greedy tightness produces an initial packing
that is typically within a few bins of the optimum, reducing both the number of
iterations required to reach a high-quality region and the frequency of costly
total-destruction events.

\subsection{Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search}
The search follows the large-neighborhood-search paradigm~\cite{bShaw} in its
adaptive form~\cite{bRopke}. Each iteration (i) \emph{destroys} the incumbent by
removing a subset $D$ of items and (ii) \emph{repairs} the partial solution by
reinserting $D$. Because repair can produce any feasible completion, the
neighborhood is implicitly exponential in $|D|$, which allows the search to escape
local optima unreachable by single-item moves. Three destroy operators form the
portfolio: \emph{random} removal of $k$ items; \emph{worst-load} removal, which
empties the least-filled bins first; and \emph{related-item} removal, which
removes a seed item together with the items closest to it in size. The destruction
radius $k$ is drawn uniformly from $[\lfloor 0.05\,n\rfloor,\lfloor 0.25\,n\rfloor]$
and is widened toward its upper bound as stagnation grows; all operators guarantee
that at least one item remains placed, preventing total destruction.

\subsection{Acceptance Criterion: Simulated Annealing}\label{sec:sa}

Candidate solutions are accepted by a simulated-annealing criterion.  Let
$\Delta = f(x') - f(x)$ denote the change in bin count relative to the
incumbent.  A candidate with $\Delta\le0$ is accepted unconditionally; a
candidate with $\Delta>0$ is accepted with probability $\exp(-\Delta/T)$.  The
temperature follows a geometric cooling schedule,
%
\begin{equation}
	T \;\leftarrow\; \alpha_{\mathrm{cool}}\,T,
	\qquad T_0 = \frac{1}{\ln 2},\quad
	\alpha_{\mathrm{cool}} = 0.9995
	\label{eq:sa}
\end{equation}
%
so that at $T_0$ a one-bin worsening is accepted with probability $0.5$.  Two
adaptive mechanisms counteract premature convergence.  A \emph{soft reheat}
raises $T$ toward $0.35\,T_0$ when the best solution has not improved for a
prescribed number of iterations.  A \emph{diversification restart}---reverting
to the best solution found, partially reheating to $0.20\,T_0$, and resetting
the stagnation counter---is triggered when the soft reheat fails to produce
improvement within a secondary patience budget.  The fixed iteration count is
the sole termination criterion; no wall-clock time limit is imposed.

\subsection{Online Learning: Warm-Start LinUCB Operator Selection}
Operator selection is delegated to a warm-start contextual bandit. During the
first $\mathrm{warmup\_calls}$ selections, a context-free Beta--Bernoulli Thompson
sampler quickly identifies promising operators, mitigating the cold-start of a
linear bandit on short runs. Thereafter a disjoint LinUCB bandit~\cite{bChu}
takes over: for a context vector $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^5$ it selects the
operator that maximizes
\begin{equation}
	\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k^\top \mathbf{x}
	+ \alpha\sqrt{\mathbf{x}^\top A_k^{-1}\mathbf{x}},
	\qquad \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k=A_k^{-1}\mathbf{b}_k,\label{eq:ucb}
\end{equation}
with the exploration weight $\alpha$ and warm-up length tuned in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. The bandit also supports a persistent state
(matrices $A_k^{-1}$, $\mathbf{b}_k$, Thompson counts) that we pre-train on a
disjoint training slice and reload at the start of every solve so that the
policy carries across instances rather than restarting from scratch. We update
$A_k^{-1}$ by the $O(d^2)$ Sherman--Morrison formula
and $\mathbf{b}_k$ by $\mathbf{b}_k\leftarrow\mathbf{b}_k+r\,\mathbf{x}$. The
context encodes the normalized temperature $T/T_0$, the stagnation progress toward
the next restart, the cost-to-bound ratio $f(x)/\mathrm{LB}_1$, the destruction
radius $k/n$, and the overall search progress. The reward is
$\min(1,\,\text{bins\_saved}/\text{gap})$ when bins are saved---normalized by the
gap to $\mathrm{LB}_1$ so that improvements near the optimum count more---$0.2$ for
an accepted non-improving move, and $0$ for a rejected one.

\subsection{Offline Learning: GBT Repair by Behavioral Cloning}
Repair reinserts displaced items in non-increasing size order. For an item, the
set of feasible bins (those with enough residual capacity) is scored by a gradient
boosted classifier~\cite{bFriedman}, and the item is placed in the highest-scoring
bin; a new bin is opened only when no bin is feasible. The model is trained offline
by behavioral cloning of BFD: each feasible (item, bin) pair is labeled $1$ if BFD
would select that bin and $0$ otherwise. Every pair is encoded by eleven features
normalized by capacity or instance size---item size and its square, size rank,
fraction of items still to reinsert, bin load, residual capacity, post-placement
slack, bin occupancy, the largest and smallest item already in the bin, and the
fill ratio. Training pools two trace types---fresh BFD replays and
post-destruction repair states---to reduce the distribution shift between offline
replay and the mid-search states encountered at inference. A standard scaler is
stored with the model, and a fast predictor applies it in NumPy and reads class
probabilities directly, avoiding per-call validation overhead. Algorithm~\ref{alg:alns}
summarizes the complete loop.

The parameters govern five distinct aspects of the solver.
$\alpha_{\mathrm{cool}}$ and $\mathrm{initial\_temperature}$ control the SA
cooling schedule; $\mathrm{reheat\_soft\_mult}$ and $\mathrm{reheat\_hard\_mult}$
set the temperature multipliers applied at soft and hard reheats respectively.
$k_{\min}$ and $k_{\max}$ bound the destruction radius, while
$\mathrm{no\_improve\_frac}$ defines the stagnation budget that triggers a reheat.
Finally, $\mathrm{bandit\_alpha}$ and $\mathrm{warmup\_calls}$ govern the LinUCB
exploration bonus and the warm-up length before the bandit takes control.


\subsubsection{Composite Objective and Instance Bank}

Each configuration is evaluated on a fixed bank of $100$ instances drawn from
five benchmark families --- Falkenauer-U, Falkenauer-T, Scholl-1, Scholl-2, and
Scholl-3 --- covering a representative range of problem sizes and structures.
The same bank is reused across all evaluations, for consistent and comparable rankings.
Configurations are scored by a composite objective that balances solution quality against computational cost:

\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:composite}
	\Phi(\theta) \;=\;
	w_q \cdot \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
	\frac{b_i(\theta) - \mathrm{LB}_{1,i}}{\max(\mathrm{LB}_{1,i},1)}
	\;+\;
	w_t \cdot \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
	\frac{t_i(\theta)}{\max(\tau,10^{-9})},
\end{equation}

\noindent where $b_i(\theta)$ is the bin count, $\mathrm{LB}_{1,i}$ the continuous lower bound, $t_i(\theta)$
the wall-clock time, and $\tau=60$\,s the time normaliser. Weights $w_q=0.9$ and $w_t=0.1$ prioritise solution
quality while penalising impractically slow configurations. The relative gap formulation makes the quality
term scale-invariant across instances of different sizes.


\subsubsection{Three-Stage Pipeline}

The pipeline balances broad exploration with precise refinement through a structured
three-stage handoff. Stage~0 evaluates parameter groups independently to construct
an isolation anchor, which seeds Stage~1's global Bayesian search without restricting
its domain. Stage~1 then hands off to Stage~2 by physically contracting the search
boundaries around its best result.

\paragraph{Stage~0: Isolation Grid Search.}
Parameters are split into three functional groups (SA thermal, destruction, and bandit)
and grid-searched independently, with all others held at their defaults. The best
setting from each group is merged into the \emph{isolation anchor}, passed to Stage~1
as a seed.

\paragraph{Stage~1: Coarse Bayesian Search.}
TPE explores the full search space, seeded by both the solver defaults and the
isolation anchor. Structurally invalid pairs (e.g.\ $k_{\min} \ge k_{\max}$) are
pruned before consuming any evaluation budget.

\paragraph{Stage~2: Fine Bayesian Search.}
The search boundaries are contracted to a $\pm15$--$25\%$ window around the Stage~1
best. TPE refines within this focused basin to produce the final tuned parameter set.

		\STATE $x \leftarrow \textsc{BFD-WarmStart}()$;\; $x^\star \leftarrow x$
		\FOR{$t=1$ \TO $\mathrm{max\_iter}$}
		\STATE $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}} \leftarrow \textsc{Context}(x,T,t)$
		\STATE $a \leftarrow \textsc{Bandit.Select}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{ctx}})$

		\ENDFOR

\section{Experiments, Results and Analysis}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Protocol and Setup}
All runs use a fixed random seed ($42$), a budget of $5000$ ALNS iterations,
the tuned configuration of Section~\ref{sec:approach} ($T_0=0.5507$,
$\alpha_{\mathrm{cool}}=0.99904$, and the destruction/bandit parameters listed
above), and a single pre-trained repair model and pre-trained LinUCB warm-start
state, both shared across every dataset. The solver is
implemented in Python~3.13 with scikit-learn~\cite{bPedregosa} for the
gradient-boosted repair model, and experiments were run on a workstation running
Windows~11 with an Intel Core i9-13950HX CPU and 64\,GB of RAM. Solution quality
is measured by the gap to the
lower bound, $\text{gap}=\text{bins used}-\mathrm{LB}_1$ of~\eqref{eq:lb}; a gap of
zero certifies optimality with respect to $\mathrm{LB}_1$.

\subsection{Datasets}
We evaluate on four widely used 1D-BPP benchmark families, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:datasets}. Scholl-2 contains uniformly distributed instances with
capacity $1000$ and $50$--$500$ items. Falkenauer-T comprises triplet instances,
where an optimal bin holds one large and two small items, at capacity $1000$.
Falkenauer-U holds uniform instances with the tighter capacity $150$. W\"ascher
contributes hard cutting-stock-style instances at capacity $10000$, and Hard28 a
set of difficult $160$--$200$-item instances at capacity $1000$.

\subsection{Parameter Tuning Results}

We run the full three-stage pipeline: a Stage~0 isolation pre-screening over
three parameter groups (SA thermal, destruction, bandit), then Stage~1 ($35$
coarse TPE trials) seeded by both the solver defaults and the isolation
anchor, then Stage~2 ($20$ fine TPE trials) within a $\pm15$--$25\%$ contracted
window around the Stage~1 best. Stage~1 and Stage~2 use $2{,}500$ ALNS
iterations per evaluation on an $18$-instance bank subset; the final
re-evaluation runs the Stage~2 configuration at $2{,}500$ iterations on a
fresh draw of $40$ instances from the full bank. Total wall-clock time was
approximately $9$\,h on $20$ parallel workers (isolation $4$\,h, Stage~1
$2.6$\,h, Stage~2 $2.2$\,h, final eval $0.04$\,h).

Table~\ref{tab:tuning} traces the pipeline from the default configuration to the
final re-evaluation. Stage~1 broadly explores the full $S_1$ ranges and reduces
$\Phi$ by $3.3\%$ over the default; Stage~2 refines within the contracted
window and reaches the lowest observed $\Phi$ of $0.0752$ on the $18$-instance
Stage~1/2 bank ($-7.3\%$ vs default). The final re-evaluation runs the Stage~2
configuration on a fresh $40$-instance draw and yields $\Phi=0.0788$
($-2.8\%$ vs the default $\Phi$ measured on the same $40$-instance bank).
Decomposing $\Phi$ shows that the tuning trades a $20\%$ \emph{worsening} of
the mean relative gap ($0.01064 \to 0.01281$ bins/$\mathrm{LB}_1$) for a
$6\%$ \emph{reduction} of the normalised time
($0.7150 \to 0.6731$); with $w_q=0.9$, $w_t=0.1$ the time gain just outweighs
the quality loss. This trade-off reflects the optimiser's prior, not the
solver's limit: a quality-only weighting ($w_t=0$) would push the search
elsewhere. The final tuned configuration, applied to all multi-dataset runs
in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, is:

\subsection{Ablation Study}
To isolate the effect of each learning component we run all four configurations
on the five full benchmark families ($685$ instances), keeping all ALNS
settings identical and toggling only the two switches
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The ablation now discriminates between configurations
in a dataset-dependent way. On Scholl-2 the dual-learning method delivers the
lowest mean gap ($0.565$ bins versus $0.590$ for the no-learning baseline) and
the highest optimum rate ($80.4\%$ vs $77.7\%$). On the Falkenauer-T triplets,
which are structurally hostile to $\mathrm{LB}_1$, the online bandit alone is
the strongest configuration ($1.288$ bins, a $15\%$ reduction over no-learning);
the offline repair model trained on Scholl-style instances does not transfer
to triplets and the combined method is slightly worse than no-learning, an
expected mismatch between training distribution and instance class. On
Falkenauer-U the offline model alone yields the lowest gap ($0.738$ bins) while
the combined method reaches the highest optimum rate ($52.5\%$ vs $42.5\%$ for
no-learning). W\"ascher and Hard28 are saturated at the present budget: all four
configurations produce identical packings, so the learning components affect
only solve time. Across the five families the combined method is never the
worst configuration in quality and improves the optimum rate on three of five
families; it is the configuration retained for the comparative study.

\section{Conclusion and Perspectives}\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a dual-learning Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search for the
one-dimensional bin packing problem that injects machine learning at two decision
points of the destroy--repair loop: an online warm-start LinUCB bandit for
destroy-operator selection and an offline gradient-boosted repair model trained by
behavioral cloning of Best-Fit Decreasing. An ablation across the five full
benchmark families ($685$ instances) shows that the two learning components
contribute in a dataset-dependent way: the dual-learning configuration is best
on Scholl-2 ($0.565$ bins vs $0.590$ for no-learning), the online bandit alone
is best on Falkenauer-T triplets ($1.288$ vs $1.512$, a $15\%$ reduction
where the offline model trained on Scholl-style instances does not transfer),
and the offline model alone is best on Falkenauer-U ($0.738$ bins). On the
comparative study the dual-learning configuration achieves the best gap
($0.05$), dominating every baseline on quality while running about
$22\times$ faster than ant colony optimization. Across the five families the
combined method reaches the optimum on $80.4\%$ of Scholl-2 and $52.5\%$ of
Falkenauer-U instances; harder families remain bounded by the per-instance
time budget. The main limitations
are the single-run reporting of stochastic baselines and the runtime overhead
of the offline model on the largest instances. Future work will extend the evaluation to full datasets and stronger
lower bounds such as the Martello--Toth $L_2$ bound, where learned repair is
expected to pay off on larger feasible-bin sets, replace the cloned repair model
with a policy learned directly from search reward, and study the transfer of the
learned components across instance families.
